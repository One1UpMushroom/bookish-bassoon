First power-up

Plug the QT Py into USB, then plug in the 24 V supply. On the lamp choose Menu ▸ Mode ▸ Mixer, then in Menu ▸ Adjust set Brightness to 100%. Measure with the black probe on a blue rail.

  1. Nothing gets warm in the first minute. If something does, unplug and look at the diode and capacitor stripes first.
  2. Board A red rail: 23–25.5 V Board B red rail: 11.5–12.5 V
  3. Strip gates. Warm at 100%: the resistor leg in A·c6 reads 3.1–3.3 V Warm at 0%: 0 V Same for Cool at A·h12.
  4. Set the RGB currents now, with nothing to damage. Red at 100%: measure the resistor leg in B·a14 (op-amp pin 3) and turn the red pot until it reads 2.50 V That presets 50 mA. Do the same for Green at B·a16 and Blue at B·j16. The pot cannot go past 3.3 V, which is 66 mA.
  5. Op-amp alive, LED strings still disconnected. With Red at 100% the resistor leg in B·c12 (pin 1) reads 10–11 V and at 0% under 0.1 V Same for Green at B·c18 and Blue at B·h18. Strings already connected? Then 100% reads about 4–5 V instead, and that is correct: see What the meter should read.
  6. Fail-safe. Unplug the QT Py's USB cable. Both strip gates must read 0 V

Stop and check · strip connected

Is 3.3 V enough for your MOSFET?

It should be: the IRLB8721 starts to conduct at 1.35–2.35 V. But its datasheet only promises a low on-resistance at 4.5 V and above, so this measurement is what settles it for your parts. This test is for the two strip MOSFETs only. The three RGB MOSFETs are never fully on, by design (why).

  1. Warm at 100%, Cool at 0%: the strip lights warm. Swap them: it lights cool.
  2. With Warm at 100%, measure across the warm MOSFET: red probe on the diode leg in A·a7, black probe on the thick wire in A·a8. under 0.10 V means it is fully on. Above 0.3 V, swap in another IRLB8721. You have 19.
  3. Repeat for Cool between A·j13 and A·j14.
  4. If your meter has a 10 A range, put it in series with the strip's + lead and note the current at Warm 100% and at Cool 100%. The firmware wants those two numbers later.
  5. After five minutes at 100% both MOSFETs should be barely warm.

Before the RGB strings · the mystery stars

Find each die and its + side

Only R and B are printed on your stars. This test finds the green pads and each die's polarity at about 10 mA.

  1. Take a 220 Ω resistor. Connect the QT Py's 5V pad through it to one pad of a pair, and the other pad of the pair to GND.
  2. No light: swap the two leads. Never use more than 5 V here. An LED only survives about 5 V the wrong way round.
  3. Mark the + pad and the colour of all three pairs on all four stars.
  4. Wire each colour as one chain: Board A red rail → star 1 + … star 1 − → star 2 + … and so on, ending with star 4 − → that colour's drain row on Board B.
  5. Tape or bolt the stars to a piece of aluminium. At 50 mA each star makes about 0.4 W.

First colour

  1. Red at 100%, the rest at 0%: all four red dies light. Measure across a red sense resistor, from the leg in B·a9 to the blue rail. It reads your preset, 2.5 V = 50 mA
  2. Dark and 0 V means a die is the wrong way round or a joint in the chain is open.
  3. Repeat for Green at B·a23 and Blue at B·j23.
  4. The op-amp outputs (B·c12, B·c18, B·h18) now read about 4–5 V, and each RGB MOSFET drops 9–13 V from drain to source. That is how a current sink works, not a weak MOSFET: see the readings table.

Why it changed, and the numbers

RGB sinks · LED strings connected · 50 mA preset

What the meter should read

The three RGB MOSFETs are not switches. The op-amp holds each one partly on, with its gate at whatever voltage passes exactly the preset current. The voltage the string does not use is dropped across the MOSFET, which is why it runs warm. The strip test (under 0.1 V across a fully-on MOSFET) does not apply to these three. The op-amp output sits 1.4–2.4 V above the sense resistor reading. That gap is the MOSFET's own gate threshold, and it varies from part to part, so at a 2.5 V preset the gate reads anywhere from 3.9 to 4.9 V. With the strings disconnected no current can flow, so the output runs up to 10–11 V instead. That is the figure on the first check card.

Fault-finding · RGB

A colour stays lit at 0%

If a colour goes completely dark at 0% and its sense resistor reads your preset at 100%, that channel works, whatever its gate reads. If it stays lit at 0%, the MOSFET is only doing what its gate is told, so find out what is telling it. Black probe on Board B's blue rail, colour at 0%:

  1. Pot setting (a14, a16 or j16) must read 0 V If it doesn't, the pot isn't being fed by the QT Py pin. Check that Menu ▸ Settings ▸ Channels ▸ (colour) ▸ Invert is Off, and that the pot's top leg is wired to the QT Py's A0, A1 or A2 pad and nothing else.
  2. Pot reads 0 V, op-amp output is high: check the op-amp's power. LM324 pin 4 (the orange wire end in B·a15) reads 11.5–12.5 V and pin 11 (the black wire end in B·j15) reads 0 V A faint glow rather than full brightness usually means a 1 MΩ goes to the blue rail instead of the red one. That resistor adds the 12 mV that holds 0% dark.
  3. Board to board. Red probe on Board A's blue rail, black on Board B's: under 0.05 V If not, the black wire between the boards is loose or in the wrong row. Fix it before lighting the RGB again: without it the LED current has no proper path back to the supply.
  4. All of that is right and it's still lit: swap the LM324 for another of your 12. When all three colours fail the same way, the cause is something they share, so swapping MOSFETs won't help.

Why 66 mA is the breadboard ceiling

From 24 V, each sink burns the voltage its string does not use. A bare TO-220 handles about 1 W before it gets too hot for a breadboard, and the sense resistors are quarter-watt parts.

66 mA (pot at maximum)0.81 W0.55 W0.11 W

Figures assume about 8.4 V across the red string and 12.4 V across green and blue. They are at 100% duty; dimming scales them down.

Later · full RGB power

The LM3414 route is unchanged

Linear sinks from 24 V waste more than half the power as heat, so 310 mA per colour still means either a heatsink on each sink MOSFET or your 25 LM3414 buck drivers. The switch-mode circuit from revision 1 stands as it was. It has not been re-checked in this revision.

  • To buy: three shielded inductors of 100–150 µH with at least 0.6 A saturation current, and three SOIC-8 adapter boards with a centre thermal pad.
  • Per colour, from inventory: LM3414 (IC-023), 1N5819, 10 kΩ on IADJ for 312 mA, 47 kΩ on FS for about 425 kHz, 1 kΩ in series with DIM and 10 kΩ from DIM to ground, 1 µF on VCC, 10 µF at VIN.
  • Wiring: string + on 24 V, string − through the inductor to LX, 1N5819 from LX up to 24 V. Build it on perfboard and check the pinout against TI's datasheet figure before soldering.
  • The PWM wires then go to the DIM inputs and the firmware stays as it is.
