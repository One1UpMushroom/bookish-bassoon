Long leg to +, stripe over the filled half. A reversed electrolytic on 24 V fails, sometimes loudly.

Step 8 of 12

The 5 V converter

1×5 V converterordered

Not sure which pin is pin 1? Test the loose module with a 9 V battery: + on the pin you believe is pin 1, − on the middle pin. The third pin then reads 5 V. Wrong way round, it reads nothing.

Pin 1 is its input and goes in the square pad marked IN. The three pins are in a row, so it also fits the wrong way round. Find pin 1 from the drawing in its datasheet before you solder.

Three stages

Power-up and wiring

!
Stop and check · nothing plugged in

First power-up

No QT Py, no strips, no display. Connect the 24 V supply to the input terminal: + and − are printed beside it. Black probe on the GND test pad.

  1. Nothing gets warm in the first minute. If something does, unplug and look at the diode stripes and the capacitors first.
  2. Either +24 screw: 23–24 V That is the supply less the half volt the SB560 takes.
  3. The +5V test pad: 4.9–5.1 V If it reads anything else, stop here. The next thing to be plugged in is the QT Py, and this is the voltage it gets.
  4. Top-right hole of the QT Py socket, marked 5V: 4.6–5.0 V

Step 11 of 12

Supply and strips

1×24 V supply1.5 A or more
1×RGB strip, 0.8 minventory LED-010
1×White strip, 1 myou have it

Cut the strips only on the marked copper pads. The RGB strip can be cut every 100 mm, so 800 mm is eight segments. The display and encoder in the picture are for step 12.

Unplug the supply first. Screw the RGB strip’s four wires and the white strip’s three wires into their terminals. Go by the letters printed on the strip at its cut line, since wire colours vary between makers.

!
Stop and check · strips connected, QT Py still out

Everything stays dark

Plug the supply back in.

  1. No colour lights, not even faintly. The 100 kΩ resistors are holding all five MOSFETs off.
  2. A colour that lights fully has a solder bridge between the middle and right pins of its MOSFET. A faint glow means a 100 kΩ is missing or badly soldered.

Step 12 of 12

QT Py, display and encoder

1×QT Py RP2040you have it
1×TFT displayyou have it
1×Rotary encoderyou have it

The QT Py needs firmware 1.2.0. Load it over USB before or after it goes into the board. USB and the 24 V supply can be connected at the same time.

Unplug the supply again. Plug in the QT Py with its USB socket toward the edge of the board. Run eight wires from J4 as the table below shows, and plug the encoder’s cable into the socket on the end of the QT Py.

!
Stop and check · everything connected

First light

Plug in the supply. The display starts and the lamp fades up.

  1. One colour at a time. In Menu ▸ Settings ▸ Channels open Red, Green, Blue, Warm and Cool in turn. Only that colour lights. If another one does, two wires are swapped at the terminal.
  2. Is each MOSFET fully on? In Menu ▸ Mode ▸ Mixer set one colour to 100 %. Red probe on that colour’s screw, black on GND: under 0.10 V The gates are driven from 3.3 V, which this MOSFET handles easily at these currents, but its datasheet only promises 4.5 V, so each one is checked once. One that reads higher gets swapped for another from the bag.
  3. A colour that never lights: its MOSFET is in backwards, or its 220 Ω is not soldered.
  4. After five minutes with everything at 100 %, the MOSFETs are cold and the SB560 is warm. That is normal.
Once, when it all works

Calibrate

  1. Settings ▸ Channels ▸ each colour ▸ Min duty. The lamp shows only that colour at its dimmest. Lower Min duty until the light goes out, then go back up one step.
  2. Settings ▸ Power. The currents already entered are catalogue figures: 133 mA for each RGB colour, 300 mA for each white. If you can measure the real ones, enter them. Set Budget to about 80 % of your supply’s rating.

The circuit board

Ordering from PCBWay

  1. Choose PCB Instant Quote and upload tasklamp-driver-gerbers.zip from the repository’s hardware/tasklamp-driver folder. The size, 96.5 × 59 mm, and the two layers are read from the file.
  2. Keep the standard options: FR-4, 1.6 mm thick, 1 oz copper, HASL finish, any solder-mask colour. Nothing on the board needs a special process.
  3. Quantity 5 is the minimum.
  4. Compare PCBWay’s preview with the picture at the top of this page before paying.

The mounting holes are 3.2 mm, for M3 screws, on a 88.9 × 50.8 mm rectangle. No board has been built from these files yet, so before ordering, print the top layer at full size and hold the fuse holder, converter and terminals against it.
