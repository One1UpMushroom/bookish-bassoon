Task Lamp Driver Build

You can drive both LED loads from parts you already own. The tunable-white strip runs at full power today. The four RGB stars run at a breadboard-safe current today, and reach full power once you add heatsinks or build the switch-mode version at the end of this page.

Channels WW and CW · build twice

Strip switch

The strip only needs its return wire switched. The IRFZ44N in your parts is not a logic-level MOSFET: it needs about 10 V on its gate to switch fully, and a 3.3 V pin leaves it half on and hot. One LM339 comparator turns the 3.3 V PWM into a 12 V signal. A BC547 emitter follower charges the gate in about a microsecond, and a 1N5819 empties it back into the comparator output.

Keep the strip current off the breadboard. Breadboard contacts are rated around 1 A. Solder the strip's WW− and CW− wires straight to each drain leg. Run a 22 AWG or heavier wire from each source leg to the supply's − terminal, and feed the strip's + from the supply's + terminal.
Channels R, G, B · build three times

Current sink

The op-amp adjusts the MOSFET until the voltage across the 2.5 Ω sense resistor equals its reference. The reference comes from the PWM pin itself, through a divider and a pot. When the pin is high, the string carries the set current. When it's low, the reference is zero and the MOSFET shuts off. The pot sets the current, so you can also white-balance the three colors on it.

Why the current is capped at 52 mA on the breadboard

From 24 V, the sink MOSFET burns all the voltage the string doesn't use. The red string drops only about 8–9 V, so its MOSFET burns about 15 V × the current. A bare TO-220 on a breadboard handles about 1 W, so the 220 kΩ top resistor limits the pot's range to a safe maximum.

Current = (3.3 V × 10 kΩ ⁄ (Rtop + 10 kΩ) × pot position − 12 mV) ⁄ 2.5 Ω. To set it, read the voltage across the sense resistor: 0.125 V = 50 mA.

Bring-up

Build it in this order

  1. Power section only. Connect 24 V and measure: the +12 V rail should read 11.5–12.5 V and VT about 1.79 V.
  2. Strip switches, no strip yet. With the lamp firmware running, choose Mixer mode. Warm at 100% should put about 11 V on the WW gate, and 0% should leave it under 0.5 V. Repeat for Cool. Unplug the QT Py's USB: both gates must stay low. This also checks that the marketplace IRFZ44Ns switch properly.
  3. Identify the RGB dies. On each star, feed the QT Py's 5V pad through a 220 Ω resistor into a pad, and connect the other pad to GND. That's about 10 mA, a dim and safe glow. If nothing lights, swap the two leads. Don't use more than 5 V for this test, because LEDs only tolerate about 5 V in reverse. Mark which pad pair is red, green and blue, and which side is +. Only R± and B± are printed on your stars.
  4. Wire the three strings. Chain LED 1 R− → LED 2 R+ → LED 3 R+ … → LED 4 R−, and the same for green and blue. Turn all three pots fully toward the ground end, then connect each string's last − pad to its MOSFET drain.
  5. Set the currents. In Mixer, set Red to 100% and the others to 0. Turn the red pot until the sense resistor reads 0.125 V (50 mA). Do the same for green and blue. After 5 minutes, check that no MOSFET is hotter than the red one.
  6. Connect the strip. Put your meter in series with the strip's + lead on the 10 A range. Read the current at Warm 100%, then at Cool 100%, and write both down.
  7. Enter the settings below, then calibrate Min duty on each channel.

Not in the inventory

  • A 24 V DC supply, 3 A or more and class 2, which the strip datasheet requires. Add a barrel-jack-to-screw-terminal adapter.
  • 22 AWG or heavier hookup wire for the strip's + and returns. Use your breadboard and jumper wires for everything else.
  • For full RGB power: an aluminum heatsink and thermal paste under the four stars (about 12 W in total). You'll need these whichever driver you use.
Later · full RGB power at 90% efficiency

Switch-mode version with your LM3414s

You have 25 LM3414 constant-current buck drivers. One per color replaces the op-amp sink. Each chip sets its own current, takes the same PWM on its DIM pin, and wastes about 10% instead of half. At full current the RGB side then draws about 0.5 A from 24 V instead of 0.94 A, with no hot transistors. The firmware doesn't change.

  • From your inventory, per color: LM3414 (IC-023), 1N5819 (D-002), 10 kΩ ×2, 47 kΩ, 1 kΩ, 1 µF ceramic (C-027), and 10 µF ceramic (C-030) at VIN. Add a 1 µF ceramic across the LED string if you want to smooth the ripple.
  • To buy:
    • 3 shielded inductors, 100–150 µH, with saturation current of 0.6 A or more and resistance under 0.5 Ω.
    • 3 SOIC-8 adapter boards that include a center thermal pad, or a small PCB. The chip's exposed pad must be soldered to ground copper.
  • Build it on perfboard, not the breadboard. At 425 kHz, the VIN capacitor, the diode and the LX node must sit within a few millimetres of the chip. Before soldering, check the pinout and LED/inductor order against the typical-application figure in TI's LM3414 datasheet.
  • Firmware afterwards: set Power ▸ R, G, B @100% to the current drawn from 24 V, about 130 mA for red and 200 mA each for green and blue.
